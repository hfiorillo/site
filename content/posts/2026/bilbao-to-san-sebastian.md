Earlier this year, me and 2 friends decided that we were going to run from Bilbao to San Sebastian. Eating and drinking our way across the North of Spain taking enough stuff to sleep in BnBs along the way. The total distance was around 126km with around 3000m of elevation.

## Leg 1: Bilbao to Guernica (30km, 958m)

In hindsight this was probably the easiest day. The weather was BRILLIANT. The start was a bit of a grind though — steep tarmac roads and endless stairs out of Bilbao before we finally hit the trail and things got fun.

We also got our first taste of the region’s guard dogs (see pic of biggest one) at almost every building, barking at you the entire way past.

To finish off the day, I threw up pretty much as soon as we arrived as my blood sugars fell off the planet.

## Leg 2: Guernica to Deba (50km, 1700m)

Definitely the hardest day.. not a great start, when we woke up all our clothes were soaked through after washing them last night. We spent a good while hair drying all our running kit, fun!

Route booted off with a nice slow climb out of Guernica where we saw a man chase some horses with a stick trying to get them to eat bread. Then, quite simply the heavens opened and remained open for the rest of the day. We got soaked. The weather wasn’t so BRILLIANT on this day.

We met a few people along the route who thought we were mad doing today’s route due to a section they described as ‘up, down, up, down’ and needing a rope ¿

So we spent the day wondering about this rope, questioning when it would come and dreading the moment… it never came, our only conclusion being that frenchmen pulled some kind of sick prank on some simple english boys.

<img src="/public/images/posts/bilbao-to-san-sebastian/IMG_0303.jpg" alt="Gorgeous Txuleta" style="float:left; max-width:200px; margin:0 1rem 0.5rem 0" loading="lazy" decoding="async">

Anyways, we were very happy to see the sea for the first time. We jumped and cheered but still had 15km to go so plodded on. When we got around 2km out there had been a landslide taking out a huge section of the route which forced us to back track on ourselves - not fun. But.. we were only 2km away. The feeling of relief when we arrived was something that can only be described as bliss. After doing a victory lap of Deba (nice place) we checked into our cosy 3 man hotel room, showered and head out. Finally getting our hands on a Txuleta.

## Leg 3: Deba to San Sebastián (45km, 1500m)

Our final day. What a day it was — the sun came out and remained out.

Defined mostly by going up and down, hugging the coastline, and seeing some amazing coastal towns, we kept a strong pace all day, rarely slowing despite the heat.

After a shaky start out of Deba, up a 30%+ gradient for around 2km with some navigational issues, we got back on track ASAP. The route then had us staying up in the hills for a while, and with today’s good visibility we could enjoy the views — this is what we came for!

We quickly descended into our first coastal town, Zumaia, where we grabbed a donut, pintxos, and a coffee before boosting off up over the next hill. This pattern of climbing and descending into towns continued throughout the day. We passed the ancient town of Getaria and ran along the length of Zarautz beach.

The last leg of the journey consisted of a stop in Orio, followed by the biggest climb of the day. At the top of which, we had a spiritual experience. A man whose name I cannot remember, but whose face I’ll never forget, greeted us with chocolate cake and water. Adam, who’d been non-verbal for the last hour, was particularly grateful.

Spirits boosted, we ploughed on. We could now see San Sebastián way off in the distance. This part of the route was particularly enjoyable, with varied terrain and great views. The next few hours flew by. Before we knew it, we were looking down at San Sebastián as the next town to descend into. A few tears of relief were shed. After a fast descent and a sprint finish down the promenade into the old town, where we were greeted by our mates, we got in the sea and enjoyed a few small beers.

We were done. 🏁🏁🏁
