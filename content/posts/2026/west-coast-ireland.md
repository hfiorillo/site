A trip around Ireland has been a bucket list trip for our group for a long time. Ireland's Wild Atlantic Way and its towns along the west coast are oh so tempting. Friends who'd had the pleasure of visiting Galway and Westport can't seem to say enough good things about the beauty of Ireland and the craic of the people who live there.

In the early planning phase, we decided we weren't going to camp on this trip. Camping would require a lot more planning, and we felt we wouldn't enjoy Ireland in the same way. It would be much harder to go to pubs all night and head back to our camping spot as a group of five. We also had some huge back-to-back days planned, where a bad night's sleep could completely kill the vibe. We also really like showers at the end of a long day of riding. Shoot me!

We did a sort of shortened version of the typical Wild Atlantic Way cycling route, covering around 800 km over the 10 days we'd planned, including travel days (of which there were a few!). Turns out getting to Ireland from Manchester by bike takes a bit of thinking about when you've got a group of five. We had issues with booking bike spots on trains and ferries. For some reason, they both seemed to have limits of four bike spaces. We didn't want to risk turning up and hoping they'd let us on: if they didn't, we'd miss a connecting journey. To avoid any potential issues with train conductors not allowing five bikes on a train, we opted to stay overnight in Holyhead the night before our ferry. It meant the five of us making our own way to the Airbnb on Thursday evening but prevented any issues with too many bikes on trains (if you can't tell, we've been stung by this before). This way, we could wake up and cycle straight to the ferry from our Airbnb, giving us a much better chance of all making it on time.

## Route

As mentioned, the route followed a mix of the [Wild Atlantic Way (WAW)](https://www.thewildatlanticway.com/route/), [EuroVelo 1](https://cycle.travel/route/summary/150702/stay) and [MizMal](https://www.mizmal.com/wild-atlantic) cycling routes. It totalled around 800 km of riding from Cork to Derry, with a ferry in between. We'd planned on cycling for seven days with a day's rest in Galway halfway through. This actually turned out to be the perfect place to stop.

We learnt a few things about Irish roads that we didn't know before heading there. Once we realised how busy the N roads (national roads) could be, we began changing our planned route on the fly to avoid them, opting for quieter L roads (local roads) instead. This was a useful distinction we hadn't really considered during the early planning stages.

When we did end up on the N roads, we found the hard shoulders, marked by broken yellow lines, useful for keeping out of the main flow of traffic. These are hard shoulders rather than dedicated cycle lanes; the [RSA's Rules of the Road](https://www.rsa.ie/docs/default-source/road-safety/r1---rules-of-the-road/rotr-book-for-web-.pdf?sfvrsn=bdef0b58_67) explain the markings and their use by pedestrians and cyclists. We experienced very little poor driving and rarely felt unsafe on the bike.

### If we had more time...

I honestly think we'd have done the full MizMal if we'd been able to get the time off work. After hearing from people who'd visited both Dingle and Achill Sound, I'd like to head back and check these places out. They look stunning.

## Packing

Since we weren't camping, we were all able to pack relatively light. That left room for more evening clothes, as we were spending a full day in Galway and planned to explore every town we stopped in, going for pints and food in each.

Overall, I think I packed well. We were catering for Ireland in the summer. I'd perhaps have brought an extra short-sleeved merino top for riding, as that was what I spent most of my time in.

## Trip highlights

- Ferries: all three of them were fun, but the [Tarbert–Killimer crossing](https://www.shannonferries.com/) was the most fun. Probably because it was the shortest journey!
- The decision to stay in B&Bs. It meant we had a nice warm bed and a shower guaranteed every night. Since we weren't racing and were instead on holiday, this was 100% the right decision for us.
- Trying to visit almost every pub in every town we went to. Irish pubs are unlike any others. It feels like they are the original pub and every other pub is trying to copy them. Pubs won't feel the same once you've visited a great Irish pub.
- The weather: in our eyes, we got unlucky. Ireland had just had two weeks of blistering sun without a drop of rain, and the day we arrived was the first wet day in weeks. So we got an authentic Irish experience.
- [Mayo v Kerry in the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship final](https://www.gaa.ie/article/all-ireland-sfc-final-mayo-triumph-at-croke-park). Arriving in Ireland, we had no idea what we were about to witness. Mayo won their first All-Ireland title in 75 years, ending a wait famously associated with a supposed [curse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_county_football_team#Curse). We witnessed one of County Mayo's finest moments and the celebrations that followed.
