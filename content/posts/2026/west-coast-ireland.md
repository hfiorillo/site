A trip around Ireland has been a bucket list trip for our group for a long time. The allure of Irelands Wild Atlanic Way and its various towns along the West Coast are oh so tempting. Friends who'd had the pleasure of visiting Galway and Wesport can't seem to say enough good things about the beauty of Ireland and the craic of the people who live there.

In the early planning phase, we decided we weren't going to camp on this trip. It would require a lot more planning and we felt we wouldn't be enjoying Ireland in the same way i.e. it would be much harder to go to pubs all night and head back to our camping spot as a group of 5. We also had some huge back to back days planned, where a bad nights sleep could completely kill the vibe. We also really like showers at the end of a long day of riding. Shoot me!

We did a sort of shortened version of the typical Wild Atlantic Way cycling route; where our route covered roughly 800km+ over the course of the 10 days we'd planned, including travel days (of which there were a few!). Turns out getting to Ireland from Manchester by bike takes a bit of thinking about when you've got a group of 5. We had issues with booking bike spots on trains and ferries. For some reason, they both seemed to have limits of 4 people max. We didn't want to risk turning up and hoping they'd let us on as if they didn't, this would mean missing out on a connecting journey. To avoid any potenital issues with train conductors not allowing 5 bikes on a train, we opted to stay overnight in Holyhead the night before our ferry. It meant the 5 of us making our own way to the airbnb on Thursday evening but prevented any issues of too many bikes on trains (if you can't tell, we've been stung by this before). This way we would be waking up and cycling to ferry from our airbnb - it would guarantee could all make it to the ferry on time.

## Route

The route itself, like previously mentioned, followed a mix of the [Wild Atlantic Way (WAW)](https://www.thewildatlanticway.com/route/), [Eurovelo 1](https://cycle.travel/route/summary/150702/stay) and "MizMal" cycling routes. Totalling around 800km of riding from Cork to Derry with a ferry inbetween. We'd planned on cycling for 7 days with a days rest in Galway halfway through. This actually turned out perfect place to stop.

We learnt a few things about Irish roads that we didn't know before heading there, once we realised that N roads were busy A-road-like roads (aka Primary Roads) we began to change our originally planned route on the fly to specifically avoid these and instead go via the less busy and quiet L roads which more akin to B-roads (aka Local Roads). This proved really useful knowledge which we'd not really considered in our early planning stages of the route.

Despite being forced onto the N roads at times, these busier roads always had a broken yellow line for their hard shoulder which you're able to use as a cycle lane. They are infact designated as such. This meant you were out the way of the traffic and could hug the side of the road as much as possible. We had very few incidents of poor driving or feeling unsafe on the bike.

### If we had more time...

I think we'd have honestly done the full MizMal if we were able to get the time off work. After hearing from people who'd visited both Dingle and Achill Sound, I'd like to head back and check these places out. They look stunning.

## Trip Highlights

- Ferry's: the 3 we had. They were fun, the shortest one from Tarbet to Killimer provided the most fun. Probably because it was the shortest journey!
- The decision to stay in bnbs. It meant every night we had a nice warm bed and shower guaranteed. Since we weren't racing and were instead on holiday, this was 100% the right decision for us.
- Trying to visit almost every pub in every town we went to. The Irish pubs are unlike any other, it feels like they are the original pub and every other pub is trying to copy them. Pubs won't feel the same once you've visited a great Irish pub.
- The weather: Whilst in our eyes we got unlucky; Ireland had just had 2 weeks of blistering sun without a drop of rain. The day we arrived was the first time they'd seen wet weather in weeks. So we got an authentic Irish experience.
- Mayo v Kerry in the All-Ireland Gaelic Football cup. Coming into Ireland we had no knowledge of what we were about to witness. This was the first time Mayo won the All-Ireland cup in 75 years, the county and everyone in Ireland believe they were [cursed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_county_football_team#Curse). We witnessed one of county Mayos finest moments and the celebrations that ensued.
