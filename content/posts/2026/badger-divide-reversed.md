The 8 of us planned (I mean seriously planned) to ride the Badger Divide on the early May bank holiday weekend.
A bit of risk if you know how turmulative Scottish weather can be, especially in these spring months but heading in May means you can hopefully avoid the midges. Which we did!
However, doing so gave us enough time to perfect our packlists and bike set ups - a key and mostly enjoyable part of any bike packing trip.
We didn't want a situation that caught us out, some parts of the trip were quite remote within the central highlands.
Whilst, ultimately, we knew we were never too far away from a road or a hotel, we didn't really want to be in a situation where we were forced to use one.

## The plan

The plan really was to enjoy it.
We were doing in a group of 7 with varying levels of ability.
This would definitely be some of the hardest riding any of us had done.
We aimed then to complete it in 5 days: camping the 1st, 3rd, 4th night and staying in a BnB for the 2nd night.

## The route

The Badger Divide runs from Glasgow to Inverness (or reverse, as we did it) through some of the most remote and beautiful landscapes in Scotland.
It traces right through the middle of the highlands past various glens and lochs, through small towns and villages.
The route totals 348km off-road with 5330m of climbing.

## Bike, bike bags and packlists

My full packlist, bike details and bag information is available [here](https://lighterpack.com/r/tjgsqt).
We all travelled on gravel bikes with a range of tyre widths - from 35mm to 2.1in MTB tyres. 
All were adequate.
As always the wider the more comfy and that was true for some of the more bumpy bits.
The bike I used a Brother Mehteh in Size XS. Running 2.1" 650b Vittoria Mezcals and SRAM Rival AXS XLPR.
This set up has been super reliable for me over various trips and was more than adequatem even for the more technical sections of the Badger Divide which verged into MTB terroritory.

My packlist is your best bet for a full breakdown of pretty much everything I took.
Getting all this stuff onto my bike is like the worlds hardest jigsaw puzzle.
It does all fit, promise.
This is what I was telling myself the night before we went after packing and unpacking for the 15th time.
All my stuff lay all over the floor, whilst i'm pondering the impossibility of it all.
Everything does have its place, the more you pack and unpack this natural resting place becomes clear.
Eating your way through your food tends to help too.

With an extra-small bike and the need to pack a tent big enough to fit 2 people, I've struggled at times to fit everything onto my bike.
The key to all my bike bag woes has been my [Tailfin](https://www.tailfin.cc), i've got the alloy pannier rack with the 18L bag.
Its a cavern and perfect for these sorts of trips.
I've tried variety of racks and large saddle bags but nothing compares to the stability and spaciousness the Tailfin has provided for me.

Rough packing arrangements:
- Handlebar bag: Quilt, sleeping mat, sleeping clothes, inflatable pillow (a must-have).
- Frame bag: Toiletries, electrics, spares, tent pegs, layers I need quick access to.
- Top tube bag: Lube, bike lock, snacks
- Snack bag: More snacks
- Tailfin: Literally everything else (Tent, spare clothes, warm layers)

## Getting there

Getting to Glasgow from Manchester with your bikes is fairly straightforward. They're frequent and cheap.
Going in a bigger group means you're likely to run into issues booking your bike reservation spots.
We've had issues before where we've not been able to get on trains without these being booked.
With that in mind, for this trip we opted not to risk chancing it and all piling on the same train.
Instead, we booked 2 separate trains and all had our bike reservation tickets.
We got a train from Manchester Airport direct to Glasgow, that train ran through Manchester Piccadilly where we picked up our friends along the way.

## Day by day

### Day 1

The first day starting in Glasgow, we had great weather pretty much all day.
This was a pleasant surprise, we'd come decked out with full rain gear just in case.
We arrived midday, with our meeting spot being the outdoor shop [Tiso](https://share.google/UaozbK2SGeTlSN40q).
It had everything we needed, outdoor gear and a coffee shop in 1.
What more could you need.
We all grabbed a coffee and filled up on buttery tattie scones.
<TATTIE SCONE>
Then we were off.

The route out of Glasgow was surprisingly pleasant, we were expecting a rough start along some roads out of city.
Instead, we found ourselves winding our way along the Kelvin walkway next to the River Kelvin pretty much straight away. 
Eventually, joining the West Highland way with the Highlands in our sights.
Our first stop was the [Beech Tree](https://maps.app.goo.gl/VAfP8LeFk1hpYfsE6) which provided a much needed water refill, coke and some cake.
After that, we didn't stop much at all. We meanered through Gartmore atop some top tier gravel, up and into the forest.
Past a waterfall.
Saw a dead snake.
Then into Aberfoyle, a quick stop at the coop and there were already talks of what we were having for dinner.
Callander had a fish and chip shop that was closing at 8pm, we had just enough time to get there and order our food before finding a camp spot for the night.
Coming into Callander we went past our first loch, Loch Venachour. A huge loch that was popular with campervans, watersports and people were swimming.
The moment we arrived in Callander, we scoffed our faces with 2 fish and a large chips each, accompanied by a soft drink of your choosing.
Sat in the centre on Callander and reflected on the day.

Prior to the trip we'd booked a [camping permit](https://www.lochlomond-trossachs.org/things-to-do/camping/get-a-permit/) for the region around Callander, which at £4.50 was reasonable.
It meant we were able to camp alongside Loch Venachour. 
After a bit of camp site hunting, we found a nice spot right next to the loch in some woods.
We weren't alone however, there was a large group camping around 100 meteres away from us.
They were blasting some questionable music out their speaker till around 11pm.
