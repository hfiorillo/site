I've always had [weblogs](https://archive.unescwa.org/weblog), all throughout my professional career. My facisination with them began from an early age. As a kid, I remember making my own personal site (hazza1998.com or something similar) using a site builder with my friend (around the time we were playing Runescape every night on our family desktop computers on Miniclip). I seem to remember the site mainly consistent of a random assortment of graphics from games we loved at the time (Rockstar Games, Max Payne, The Warriors etc.) as well as random funny gifs - it looked like your classic early 2000s website. Back then it was a chance to show off the things you liked in the way you wanted, like hanging posters in your room and decorating it how you wanted. I can't remember the name of the site we used to build that (maybe Wix or something lol). From then onwards though, i've loved them.
So naturally, from the moment I first began programming I tried to attempt to build myself a site.
Typically though, I never got very far. Often over thinking how it was built or thinking that I didn't have the knowhow so they were often abandoned.
At the start of my professional career I was using generators like Hugo and Ghost had a variety of those where I wrote and shared some basic blog posts. They can still be found on my archive posts, I've sinced migrated them all over to this site however. These mostly satiated my appetite for a webblog and came with cool themes, that were almost what I wanted but not quite. But I managed.
Around 2024, there was a huge amount of hype around Golang and [Templ](https://templ.guide/). Which at the time was a new and inguiniative way of writing Go components that render to HTML.
It was sort of like the step above the static site generators I was used to whilst also giving me freedom to design the site how I liked.
Using HTMX alongside Tailwind CSS & DaisyUI was a really low cost and fast way of generating a high quality site. That is what this site uses today.

Built with Go, templ, Tailwind CSS, and daisyUI. Source code available on [GitHub](https://github.com/hfiorillo/site).
