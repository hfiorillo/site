I've always had [weblogs](https://archive.unescwa.org/weblog), all throughout my professional career.  
As a kid, I remember making my own personal site (hazza1998.com or something similar) using a site builder with my friend, which in a way was the beginning of my obsession with them.
There was something about adding whatever you liked to the internet for anyone to be able to access that facinated me.
I seem to remember the site mainly consistent of a random assortment of gifs from games we loved at the time and pretty much anything else we thought was cool at the time - it looked like your classic generic early 2000s website. This site https://cyber.dabamos.de/88x31/ is essentially a dream for 11 year old me.
This affinity for personal sites continued into my teens, often stumbling across someones site who'd created a tutorial on how to do a specific Runescape quest.
So naturally, from the moment I first began programming the first thing I attempted was to build myself a site.
I followed various tutorials and was writing in raw HTML at the time - its safe to say I didn't get very far. I then pivoted to using site generators like Hugo and Ghost. Which I actually served me really well. I learnt how to deploy these sites (as cheap as possible!) and built up a decent workflow.
This was where I posted my early blogs which can still be found on my archive posts.

Around 2024, I was at a point where I had been writing Go daily for the past few years and felt the need to essentially write my own website.
Enter Golang + [Templ](https://templ.guide/). and [HTMX](https://htmx.org/). 
There was a huge amount of hype around this combination of software and it promised to be easy to write, fast and cheap to host, great.
I'd built a internal Platform UI tool for our developers which filled a huge gap at the time for us with the intention of managing secrets - we were on a thin budget devwise and so couldn't afford any of the main products at the time hence us building this secrets management tool which was essentially GCP SM wrapper.
I was fairly confident in my ability and so began work on this in spare time, I took huge influence from YouTube where I was following along tutorials from the likes of [Anthony GG](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jI3iL4c7D1Y) who demonstrated the full extent of what you could achieve using this combination.
Overtime, I've added to it here and there. Its come a long way since I first started adding to it. In this last year alone.
Theres still so much to do!

Its just a bit of fun for me at the moment, it provides an outlet for me to publish things I feel passionate about. So, enjoy! The site is built with Go, templ, Tailwind CSS, and daisyUI hosted on GCPs Cloud Run. Source code available on [GitHub](https://github.com/hfiorillo/site) - all the good, bad and ugly!
